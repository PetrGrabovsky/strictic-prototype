## 3. Positioning (the core, one sentence)

Slogan (Czech, verbatim): **„Maximum z času, který máš."** (= "The most out of
the time you have.") Not "lose weight", not "build your dream body" — but: you
have one, two, three hours a week and some iron at home → Strictic assembles the
most effective possible workout and measures that it works.

Supporting pillars (order = priority):

1. **Time is an input, not an obstacle.** The plan is built from time and
   equipment, not from an Instagram ideal.
2. **Honest expectations.** An hour a week isn't enough for miracles — it's
   enough for measurable progress. The app says so plainly (and that's how it
   differs from 95% of fitness marketing). NEVER use "absolute maximum",
   "guaranteed", "best in the world" — hype is a denial of your own brand.
3. **Progress is measured, not promised.** Automatic weight progression, charts,
   history.
4. **At home, offline, no gimmicks.** No ads, no calorie surveillance, no spam.

Target segments (who the story lands with):

- **Parents 30–45 with sedentary work** (= Petr's story 1:1; primary).
- **Formerly active** (sport/army/youth) — they know how to train, they lack a
  system and time.
- **Beginners with their first dumbbells** — they need guidance, not an
  encyclopedia.

## 4. SEO — the content engine (Phase 1.5+, after launch)

The landing = 1 conversion page. Organic traffic is carried by content pages,
each for its own long-tail query, each with a contextual CTA into the app.
Candidates (priority per intent and competition):

1. "Home training with adjustable dumbbells — a complete guide" (the app's core
   keyword)
2. "Is an hour of exercise a week enough? What to realistically expect" (honesty
   as content — the brand in action)
3. "How to start training again after 30 / after a break" (the returning
   segment, Petr's story as E-E-A-T)
4. "Progressive overload made simple" (education on the app's core mechanism)
5. "A home gym under 5,000 CZK — what to buy and what's pointless" (later
   synergy with affiliate) Keywords to cover naturally: home training with
   dumbbells · home workout plan · strength training at home for beginners ·
   full-body workout at home · how often to train per week · exercise for the
   busy. E-E-A-T: a signed author with a real story + own data = exactly what
   Google wants from fitness content.

## 5. Affiliate — principles (Phase 3, DO NOT BUILD before validating the app)

Idea: recommend equipment (pull-up bar, bands, dumbbells) and supplements Petr
uses himself. Honestly doable, but only with these rules:

1. **A dual standard for recommendations: "I use it myself" + "evidence level
   stated plainly".** Creatine monohydrate: strong evidence — can be recommended
   with a clear conscience. Protein: convenient food, not magic. Omega-3 / D3 /
   magnesium: situational ("if you don't eat fish / are deficient…"). Personal
   experience alone is NOT evidence and an honest app admits it. No health
   claims (also legally necessary).
2. **Visible disclosure on EVERY link:** "We earn a commission from the
   purchase, your price doesn't change. We only recommend what I use myself." —
   The "no ads" manifesto stays true only with this; hidden commission would
   kill the whole brand. When affiliate arrives, add a sentence about it
   directly into the manifesto.
3. **Placement:** only in the recommendations section / content articles. NEVER
   inside a workout (you train there).
4. **Amazon reality:** the CZ market has no local Amazon and Czechs don't shop
   there — for the CZ market local partners make sense (GymBeam has its own
   affiliate program, Alza, etc.). Amazon (OneLink) only for EN/DE markets. A
   multilingual app ⇒ a partner per market, not one global one.
5. **When:** only once there are returning users (go/kill met) and content pages
   with traffic. Earlier it has nothing to earn and only distracts.
